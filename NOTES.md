# One-Line Office Joke — line selection notes

Competition: `u-pucuys-one-line-office-joke-24-hour-sprint-aaf86162`
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, criteria weighed **in this order**:
1. it really is a one-line joke about office life
2. it is funny — punchline surprises and pays off the setup in a way anyone who has sat in an office recognises
3. it is one line — a single sentence, or setup and punchline on one line, no rambling
4. it is fresh — a specific detail of office life, not a generic complaint or a joke everyone has heard
5. it is clear on first read — no explanation needed, wording tight

All 14 candidates below are original lines written for this entry. None copies
a known joke verbatim, and none copies any house anchor/rung text. The
meeting-that-should-have-been-an-email angle was deliberately avoided: the
brief itself uses it as its example, so it is the most tired angle in the field.

## Read-aloud test (top 3)

- **#1** — "My boss calls it a 'quick sync' the way my dentist calls it 'a little
  pinch'." Read aloud, the joke lands on the last two words, "little pinch".
  Setup uses the specific, recognisable office detail "quick sync". No word is
  wasted; 77 characters.
- **#2** — "My performance review said I need to be more visible, so I now attend
  every meeting in a hi-vis vest." Lands on "hi-vis vest". Setup uses the
  specific detail "performance review". Tight at 101 characters.
- **#3** — "I set my status to 'in a meeting' so often that my own calendar now
  invites me to meetings with myself." Lands on "meetings with myself". Setup
  uses the specific detail of a status message. Tight at 103 characters.

## Winner

**#1: "My boss calls it a 'quick sync' the way my dentist calls it 'a little pinch'."**

Ordered comparison against the rest:

1. **Real office joke** — every candidate passes this, but #1's subject is the
   boss's own office jargon, so it cannot be mistaken for a caption or a stray
   observation; #8 and #13 are the weakest here (observations with a punchline).
2. **Funny** — #1's payoff is the sharpest surprise in the list: the reader
   expects a complaint about long meetings and instead gets a dentist, and the
   comparison instantly clicks because both phrases are soothing lies. #2's
   hi-vis vest and #5's poster-developers are close, but #1's twist reframes
   the whole setup rather than just escalating it.
3. **One line** — #1 is a single sentence of 77 characters, the tightest of the
   top tier; #5 (125) and #13 (122) ramble by comparison.
4. **Fresh** — "quick sync" is a specific, current office phrase, and the
   dentist analogy is a genuinely new twist on it. #6, #8, #9, #10 and #13 all
   lose here: printer jokes, reply-all chains, Final_FINAL files, "work best
   under pressure" and thermostat wars are all jokes everyone has already heard.
5. **Clear** — #1 needs no explanation and no reference the reader cannot
   follow; every word earns its place.

#2 is the runner-up: its hi-vis punchline is strong and fresh, but its setup
("be more visible") is a slightly less universal office moment than the
"quick sync" that every office worker has survived, and its payoff is a
visual gag rather than a reframing of the setup. #3 is third: the
meetings-with-myself escalation is fresh and funny, but it escalates the
premise instead of surprising with a twist, so its punchline lands softer.
